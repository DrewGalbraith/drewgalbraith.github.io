Early in the winter, the inhabitants
of Nordland begin
to get ready for
the Lofoten fisheries.
For hundreds

these
islands, there are busy scenes in homes
and
shops

Everybody
works
as if

it does,
as thousands of families de-
pend upon

to pay

Nets must be made and repaired.
Lines must be got in order.
There
is
running here and there
for cord,

rope,
hooks,
sinkers,
hired
help,
crews
for their boats,
trunks,
barrels,

for bait.
The last is important, as the
liners (those who fish with

where-
ever
there
is
reported
to
be
her-
ring there they hurriedly sail or row.
Then there
is the repairing
of boats,
sails, masts and oars.
In
the house the women
are not
idle.
There are great
thick stock-
ings and mittens
to

for which
two and
three double yarn
is used.
The

When

Cloth must

The wool for all this must be sheared
from
the sheep,
carded
and

Then the men's
clothes must be

paired, and some new ones made.
Provisions must be prepared, and

The
huge

ing a

mass

It is then held in the

When prepared for the

is

At the stores there

The merchant and

A great deal of credit is

Lofoten.
The

out
chickory and
factory,
rice and oil, syrup and nails, tea and
tobacco, cinnamon and thread, sugar

nearly everything that
is
mer-
chantable.

bered that there

The

At

porary

These

a
stove,

Butter-kegs are used for chairs,

Stormy days

Every

When

They roll

Out

In to the shore

Then the fishing appara-
